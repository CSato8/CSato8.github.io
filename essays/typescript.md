*It's Like Javascript, Kind Of*

## I Have no Idea What I am Doing Here

What's my experience with Typescript, zero. What's my experience with Javascript, basically 0. So, jumping into the deep end with Typescript was definitely a nerve wracking experience. For starters, my skills for coding are pretty weak. The only language I really know is C and maybe C++. Java is that language everyone knows and yet I still sometimes struggle with it. My only touch with Javascript is with a website I had previously made, and even then it was barely any with most of the code being HTML and CSS. So, color me surprised when my first run with Typescript wasn't awful. I could understand what the task wanted me to do and be able to translate it to code with minimal difficulties. Granted, I haven not reached the full depth and complexity that Typescript has to offer, but I am ready to meet them head on.

## It's Not That Bad

There are two ways I have thought about Typescript. There is the practical or software engineering side. In which case, I can see why Typescript is widely used instead of Javascript. Typescript offers a more readable version in the form of types. Javascript being a dynamically typed language means that values are not associated with variables and type checking is performed at execution. This can make projects a headache to maintain as values can switch types with in the same variable. Typescript offers a remedy to this issue by turning Javascript into a static typed language.

Now, for the second way I have thought about Typescript which is my personal opinion. My honest thoughts are that it's not bad. This isn't to say that I don't like Typescript, it could be way worse, it could be just Javascript, but my thoughts on most coding languages are indifferent at best.

## The Sink or Swim Approach to Learning

So, how does one go about learning Typescript? Well, it seems I have unknowingly subscribed to the "throw a child in the deep end and it'll learn to swim" approach. In a class I am currently taking, instead of lectures taking up class time they are instead replaced with a nice, fun quiz. Lectures are for students to work through during non instructional time. What does this mean for me and my fellow classmates? It means we are going to learn Typescript whether we like it or not, driven by the fear of these quizzes. Are these quizzes effective at making us learn? Probably. At least, they have scared me enough to practice frequently as to not fall behind. However, whether it will remain effective for me going forward is yet to be seen.
